# How to Tell if Your Underwire is Sitting on Breast Tissue: A Visual Guide

A well-fitting underwire bra should feel like a supportive, comfortable cradle for your breasts. It should *never* pinch, poke, or cause bruising. If you are experiencing pain, particularly on the outer sides of your chest near your armpits, you are likely suffering from a very common fit issue: your underwire is sitting on breast tissue. 

This happens when the physical wire of the bra is too narrow to encapsulate your entire breast, forcing the hard metal to rest on sensitive glandular tissue instead of your ribcage. 

## The Anatomy of a Good Fit: The Breast Root

To understand why your underwire is poking you, you must first understand the "breast root." 

The breast root is where your breast tissue attaches to your chest wall. 
*   **Narrow Roots:** The breast tissue is attached to the front of the chest and does not wrap around the sides.
*   **Wide Roots:** The breast tissue wraps further around the sides of the ribcage, sometimes extending completely under the armpit.

Bra manufacturers create underwires in different widths. If you have a wide root but you purchase a bra with narrow wires (which is common in many unlined plunge styles), the wire simply won't be wide enough to surround your breast. Instead, the edge of the wire will sit directly *on* the outer edge of your breast tissue. 

## Visual Signs Your Underwire is on Breast Tissue

How can you tell for sure if your underwire is too narrow? Look for these signs in the mirror:

1.  **The Indentation Test:** When you take your bra off at the end of the day, look at the red marks left by the underwire. The marks should sit exactly on your ribcage, completely outside of any breast tissue. If the red marks cut across the side of your breast, the wire is sitting on tissue.
2.  **The Poke:** If you press gently on the outer edge of the underwire (near your armpit), do you feel hard rib bone beneath it, or soft, fleshy breast tissue? It should rest on the bone.
3.  **The Armpit Bulge:** If breast tissue is escaping out the side of the bra and sitting behind the underwire towards your back, the wire is not encapsulating you properly. (Note: This is different from the "Tail of Spence," a natural pad of fat near the armpit that many bras don't cover). 
4.  **The Floating Gore:** If the center piece of the bra (the gore) doesn't tack flat against your sternum, it's often because the cups are too small/narrow, pushing the entire bra away from your chest. 

## The "Scoop and Swoop" Solution

Before you throw your bra away, you must ensure you are putting it on correctly. The vast majority of women do not "scoop and swoop." 

When you put your bra on, lean forward and use your opposite hand to scoop all of your breast tissue from your back/armpit area directly into the cup. If, after scooping, your breasts are spilling out of the top or the underwire still cuts into the side, the bra is definitively too small or narrow.

## How to Fix It

If you have confirmed that your underwire is resting on breast tissue, you need to change your size or style. 

1.  **Go up a cup size:** The most common reason for narrow wires is wearing a cup size that is too small. Going from a 34D to a 34DD will increase the width of the underwire, allowing it to encapsulate your wide root. 
2.  **Check your band size:** If your band is too large, it won't hold the wires firmly against your body, allowing them to shift and ride up onto your tissue. 
3.  **Change bra styles:** Some bra styles naturally feature wider wires. Half-cup bras and many molded t-shirt bras have wider wires than unlined balcony bras. 

## Stop Suffering in Silence

A poking underwire is not something you just have to "put up with." It is a clear mechanical failure of the bra's fit.
