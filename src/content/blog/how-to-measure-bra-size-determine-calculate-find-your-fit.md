## Why 80% of Women Wear the Wrong Bra Size

The primary reason so many women wear ill-fitting bras comes down to two major misconceptions:

1. **Believing Bra Sizes Never Change**: Weight fluctuations, hormonal cycles, pregnancy, muscle changes, and aging cause breast tissue and ribcage circumference to shift over time.
2. **Assuming Cup Letters Are Fixed Volumes**: People assume an "A cup" is always small and a "D cup" is always large. In reality, **cup letters are ratios relative to band size** ($\text{Bust} - \text{Band}$). A **38A** bra holds more cup volume than a **30D** bra!

```
Common Sizing Mistake:
   Wearing Band Too Large (e.g. 36B instead of 32DD)  -->  Band Rides Up Back  -->  Straps Dig Into Shoulders
```

## What Tools You Need to Measure Bra Size at Home

Before starting your measurement process, prepare the following item list:

1. **A Soft Cloth Measuring Tape**: Standard tailor's measuring tape calibrated in inches or centimeters.
2. **Alternative (No Measuring Tape)**: A non-stretch string, ribbon, or phone charging cord, plus a flat desk ruler.
3. **A Full-Length Mirror**: Ensures the tape measure remains straight and parallel across your back.
4. **An Unpadded Bra**: Wear a thin, unpadded bra that lifts your tissue without pushing it up or flattening it down.

---

## Step 1: How to Measure Underbust Band Size

Your bra band provides **80% of your bra's support**. If the band is too loose, your shoulder straps will dig in and cause shoulder fatigue.

```
Step 1: Underbust (Band) Measurement
   |
   v
[ Wrap tape snugly around ribcage directly under breasts. Keep level! ]
```

1. **Position the Tape**: Wrap the measuring tape directly around your ribcage, immediately beneath your bust fold.
2. **Check Tightness**: The tape should feel snug against your skin—snugger than you want the final bra band to feel, as bra elastics stretch out over time.
3. **Keep It Level**: Look in the mirror to verify the tape is straight across your back, parallel to the floor.
4. **Record the Number**: Take a breath out and note the measurement in inches (e.g., 31.5 inches).
5. **Determine Your Baseline Band Size**:
   - If your measurement is an **even number** (e.g. 32"), that is your baseline band size.
   - If your measurement is an **odd number or fraction** (e.g. 31" or 33"), round up to the next even number or test adjacent sister sizes based on band stretch.

---

## Step 2: How to Measure Overbust Cup Size

Cup size measures how far your breast tissue projects outward from your ribcage structure.

```
Step 2: Overbust (Cup) Measurement
   |
   v
[ Wrap tape loosely around fullest part of bust. Do not press tissue! ]
```

1. **Position the Tape**: Wrap the measuring tape around the fullest part of your bust (usually level with your nipples).
2. **Keep It Loose & Gentle**: Do **not** pull the tape tight across your breasts. It should rest lightly against your tissue without indenting.
3. **Check the Mirror**: Verify that the tape isn't sagging at the back of your ribcage.
4. **Record the Number**: Note the measurement in inches (e.g., 36.5 inches).

## Step 3: Calculating Your Cup Letter Math

To calculate your bra cup size manually, subtract your Underbust Band measurement from your Overbust Bust measurement:

$$\text{Difference (Inches)} = \text{Overbust (Bust)} - \text{Underbust (Band)}$$

Match your difference to the standard US/UK cup conversion table below:

```
Difference (Inches) | US Cup Size | UK Cup Size | Description & Projection
--------------------|-------------|-------------|----------------------------
Less than 0.5"      | AAA         | AAA         | Very slight projection
0.5 to 1.0 inch     | AA          | AA          | Minimal projection
1.0 inch            | A           | A           | 1" bust-to-band difference
2.0 inches          | B           | B           | 2" bust-to-band difference
3.0 inches          | C           | C           | 3" bust-to-band difference
4.0 inches          | D           | D           | 4" bust-to-band difference
5.0 inches          | DD          | DD          | 5" bust-to-band difference
6.0 inches          | DDD / F     | E           | 6" bust-to-band difference
7.0 inches          | G           | F           | 7" bust-to-band difference
8.0 inches          | H           | FF          | 8" bust-to-band difference
9.0 inches          | I           | G           | 9" bust-to-band difference
```

### Real-World Calculation Examples:

- **Example A**: 
  - Underbust = 32 inches | Overbust = 35 inches
  - Difference = $35 - 32 = 3\text{ inches}$
  - Calculated Bra Size = **32C**

- **Example B**: 
  - Underbust = 34 inches | Overbust = 39 inches
  - Difference = $39 - 34 = 5\text{ inches}$
  - Calculated Bra Size = **34DD**

## How to Measure Your Bra Size Without a Tape Measure

If you do not own a soft tailor's measuring tape at home, use the **String & Ruler Method**:

```
[ Step 1: Wrap String Around Ribcage ]  -->  [ Step 2: Mark Pen Line ]  -->  [ Step 3: Measure Flat Against Ruler ]
```

1. Take a non-stretchy string, ribbon, shoelace, or phone charging cable.
2. Wrap it snugly around your ribcage (underbust) as described in Step 1, and mark the overlap point with a marker or pen.
3. Lay the string flat against a desk ruler or hardware measuring tape to read the inches.
4. Repeat the exact process for your fullest bust measurement (overbust).
5. Input both numbers into our [Bra Size Calculator](/#calculator).

## 4 Common Measuring Pitfalls to Avoid

- ❌ **Measuring Over Padded Bras**: Padded or push-up bras add 1 to 2 artificial inches to your cup measurement. Always measure wearing an unpadded bra or bare skin.
- ❌ **Holding Your Breath or puffing Chest Out**: Inhaling deeply makes your ribcage measurement falsely large, leading to a loose band. Exhale naturally before reading the tape.
- ❌ **Tape Drooping Across Your Back**: If the tape sags down your back, your underbust measurement will read too large. Always check your profile in a mirror.
- ❌ **Squishing Breast Tissue**: Pulling the tape tight around your bust compresses tissue, resulting in a cup size that is too small.

## Understanding Sister Sizes: The Key to Custom Fit

If your calculated size feels slightly too tight in the band or slightly gaping in the cup, **Sister Sizes** allow you to adjust band tightness while maintaining the **exact same cup volume**:

```
Baseline Size | Sister Size (Tight Band) | Sister Size (Loose Band)
--------------|--------------------------|-------------------------
34B           | 32C                      | 36A
34C           | 32D                      | 36B
34D           | 32DD                     | 36C
36C           | 34D                      | 38B
```
