    <span>How often should I re-calculate my size?</span>

  <p class="mt-4 text-body text-sm">We recommend measuring every 6 months or whenever you experience significant weight changes, pregnancy, or changes in hormonal birth control. Breast tissue density and size can fluctuate frequently.</p>

<details class="bg-surface border border-hairline rounded-xl p-5 mt-4 cursor-pointer">
  <summary class="font-bold text-ink list-none flex justify-between">
    <span>Why does the calculator ask for a leaning measurement?</span>
  </summary>
  <p class="mt-4 text-body text-sm">The leaning measurement is crucial for pendulous or sagging breasts because it allows gravity to pull all the breast tissue forward. This accurately captures your true breast volume, whereas a standing measurement often underestimates it, leading to cup sizes that are too small.</p>
</details>

<details class="bg-surface border border-hairline rounded-xl p-5 mt-4 cursor-pointer">
  <summary class="font-bold text-ink list-none flex justify-between">
    <span>Are molded t-shirt bras good for sagging breasts?</span>
  </summary>
  <p class="mt-4 text-body text-sm">Generally, no. Molded bras have a rigid, pre-formed shape. Because sagging breasts are often made of softer tissue, they tend to pool at the bottom of molded cups, leaving empty space or gaping at the top. Unlined, seamed bras are much better as they conform to and lift your natural shape.</p>
</details>

<details class="bg-surface border border-hairline rounded-xl p-5 mt-4 cursor-pointer">
  <summary class="font-bold text-ink list-none flex justify-between">
    <span>What does it mean if the top of my bra cup gapes, but the underwire digs in?</span>
  </summary>
  <p class="mt-4 text-body text-sm">This is a classic sign of a "shape mismatch" rather than the cup simply being too big. For sagging breasts, this often means the bra cup is too tall and too shallow. The underwire digs in because your breast tissue needs more depth at the bottom of the cup, pushing the wire into your ribs. Try a balconette or a seamed bra with deeper cups.</p>
</details>

<details class="bg-surface border border-hairline rounded-xl p-5 mt-4 cursor-pointer">
  <summary class="font-bold text-ink list-none flex justify-between">
    <span>How can I get lift without wearing a push-up bra?</span>
  </summary>
  <p class="mt-4 text-body text-sm">The majority of the lift in any bra comes from the band, not the cups or straps. Ensuring you are wearing a snug band that stays parallel to the floor will instantly lift your bust. Additionally, seamed cups provide structural lift from the bottom without needing extra padding.</p>
</details>
